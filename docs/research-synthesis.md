# Research synthesis: kernels and engines for DeepSeek-V4.1 decode on gfx950

Five parallel researchers (AMD libraries AITER/CK/hipBLASLt/ATOM; vLLM/SGLang/ATOM V4.1 implementations; llama.cpp/ik_llama IQ kernels; megakernels and launch overhead; formats and speculative decoding), then one synthesis. Run 2026-10-30. Gains are estimates unless a source measured them.

## Decision

Keep maya-ds4 and lift kernels and designs. Do not switch the base. No other engine can hold V4.1 on one 288 GB GPU: official MXFP4 needs about 292 GiB without Engram, and ATOM/vLLM mono accepts only TP2/TP4 (https://recipes.vllm.ai/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/60397). llama.cpp is slower than our engine at 8 users (18.3 against 33.9 tok/s/user). Our step of 29.5 ms has large gaps to the floors. Dense is 10 ms against a byte floor of about 0.7-1 ms. Routed experts are 7.7 ms against 2.2 ms. Glue and idle are 9.9 ms. So kernel efficiency limits our speed, not the weight format. Our estimate, not a measurement: the cheap fixes (dense decode-once, codebook MoE, Mega-MHC, whole-step graph) bring the step to about 18-20 ms, which is about 50 tok/s/user. To reach 100 tok/s/user, we must also run DSpark at batch 8 (AL of about 2-2.8 on our abliterated quant) and later write per-layer persistent kernels.

## Rejected options

- Switch the engine to vLLM, SGLang, ATOM or DeepInfer: official MXFP4 is about 292 GiB without Engram, which is more than 288 GB. Mono accepts only TP2/TP4. No GGUF/IQ DeepSeek path exists on ROCm (vllm-gguf-plugin has no DeepSeek map). Sources: https://recipes.vllm.ai/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm-gguf-plugin.
- Switch the base to llama.cpp: it is slower at 8 users (18.3 against our 33.9 tok/s/user, with about 2600 kernels per token). We keep it only as an MIT source for MMQ and the IQ decode routines.
- Swap in llama.cpp mul_mat_vec_q_moe for experts: it reads expert weights again for each (token, slot) pair, and on CDNA it sends IQ2 to MMQ above 5 tokens. On NVIDIA that cost 38-62% (https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/26079).
- Use ik_llama.cpp kernels or the *_R4/IQ*_KT formats: the HIP build hardcodes WARP_SIZE 32 and fails without an error on wave64 CDNA (https://github.com/ikawrakow/ik_llama.cpp/pull/2339). The KT formats give about 0 ms direct gain and need a requant from a BF16 source.
- Repack the routed experts to MXFP4, int4-g32 or MXFP6: 2.06-2.43x the bytes, the set does not fit on one GPU, and the expert floor doubles (about 2.2 -> 4.5 ms).
- Repack dense to FP8 for the AITER a16w8 asm GEMV now: about +45% bytes, asm for only 2 (N,K) shapes, and it reaches only about 2.2 TB/s at M=8. Look at it again only if action 3 still leaves the dense path limited by K-quant decode.
- Requant the experts to uniform INT2 for Humming: a quality risk on abliterated weights, out of tree, and only one source for the speed numbers (https://github.com/vllm-project/humming/issues/181).
- QTIP / AQLM trellis formats: QTIP is GPL-3.0 (do not copy), there is no gfx950 port, and the direct step gain is about 0.
- Dynamic top-k / expert skipping: 0.5-1 ms at most. It costs quality (top-6 -> 3 gave -17%) and lowers DSpark acceptance on a model that is already at about 1.8 bpw.
- ParetoQ-style QAT: we cannot train 543B routed params on one GPU. Out of scope.
- Full-model megakernel (Fleet/MPK/Hazy): months of work, and it gives only about 1.13-1.16x more than per-layer persistent kernels at bs 1-16. Fleet supports only BF16 dense Qwen3-8B. MPK is CUDA only.
- Redline / custom AQL submission: only RDNA data, experimental, and it has open VM-fault reports.
- EcoSpec cost-aware verify code: CC BY-NC-SA paper with no code, and at batch 8 tree methods were slower than AR. Use only the idea, after DSpark works.
- Lift the AITER Gluon sparse-MLA or asm mla_decode_fwd_v4_nm now: no absolute us numbers for our shape, it needs a Triton/Gluon AOT path or the aiter loader, and the asm PR was closed unmerged. Measure our 3.5 ms attention breakdown (core, indexer, top-512, 2048-block candidate stage) first, and then decide on the FP4 index plane.
- AITER fused add+RMSNorm+quant: it helps only if the next GEMM reads int8/fp8 activations, and our GGUF path reads bf16/q8. Mega-MHC (action 4) covers the larger fusion.

## Claims not verified

- AITER MXFP4 MoE at about 5.4 TB/s at M=8: the reporter computed this from 78.9 us and an assumed 45 unique experts. AITER did not measure it, and it is at TP2 (I=1152), not our full I=2304.
- Our routed-expert target of 3-3.5 ms with an IQ grid decoder: no source measured an IQ1/IQ2 codebook decoder on CDNA.
- The __byte_perm fix made IQ4 decode about 15x faster on RDNA3 (gfx1101) only. One source. We do not know that our compat layer has this problem.
- The MMQ speedups of 1.34-1.76x are from MI250X (gfx90a) at the model level. There is no gfx942/gfx950 data, and the maintainers reused the MI250X thresholds.
- The dense gain of 3-4.5 ms and the mono gain of 3-6 ms are reporter estimates, not measurements. The two gains overlap partly.
- mHC took 2.05 ms per step and about 85% of launches in vLLM before the fix (one source, vLLM issue #56506).
- DSpark AL on our abliterated ~2-bit target is not known. The 3.51 value is a synthetic golden AL. Real measurements are 2.02 (SGLang c8, V4-Flash), 2.817 (GSM8K, V4.1) and 1.85-1.89 (thinking, T=1).
- The effect of abliteration on drafter acceptance: no source gives a number.
- sgl-amd-mono c1/c4/c8 = 650/414/312 tok/s/user, and about 331 after PR #30 (one source each).
- ryanzhou single-MI300X V4-Flash numbers (C8 53.83 tok/s/user, 79.80 with DSpark): author-only, with no independent reproduction.
- The Humming MI355X speedups (4.1-4.3x dense, 1.2-2.3x MoE against AITER): one source.
- FlyDSL export to linkable C objects for a C++ hipModuleLoad path is not confirmed.
- License gaps: report 1 gives FlyDSL as Apache-2.0, but reports 3 and 4 did not check it. The tokenspeed, DeepInfer, Hazy, Mirage and Redline licenses are not checked. hipBLASLt (rocm-libraries) as MIT is not checked. The aiter MIT license comes from the repo listing, not from the LICENSE text.
- HIP graph floor of about 1.2 us per chained node: MI300X (gfx942) data only. No gfx950 number.
- Published BF16 abliterated safetensors (a lossless source for any requant) are not confirmed.
- The idea that our per-GPU byte load matches TP2 MXFP4 and so makes 100 tok/s/user realistic is an inference from the reports, not a measurement.
- The reports do not agree on which layers are compressor/indexer layers (report 2 lists 0,1,2,8,14,20,24,28,32,36; report 4 lists 2,8,14,20,24,28,32,36). Check this against our model config.
